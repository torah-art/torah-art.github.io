Understanding the Language of the Bible: Look to the Root

2015 · Art Midrash, The Language of the Bible

It is no secret that no translation, however good and anointed, can convey all the depth and the many meanings of the original text. There are places in Scripture where a Hebrew word cannot be translated in only one way. By choosing just one of the meanings, a translation brings a single meaning where there is none. That is why there is no alternative to studying the sacred texts in the original language.

To study the Hebrew original of the Bible I use STRONG'S NUMBERS, and I would like to share a few simple ways of working with them. It is a wonderful tool for those who cannot tell Hebrew letters apart at all, for those who know the basics, and all the more for those who are advanced in Hebrew.

I use the program "Bible Quote" (BibleQuote). In my opinion it is one of the best Bible programs, and it is also completely free. Links for downloading the program and its modules are at the end of the article.

How to find out the meaning of a Hebrew word in the original text of the Tanakh?

For deeper study you will need Hebrew. Knowing the alphabet and being able to read with the vowel points will already take you a long way in studying the original text of Scripture, and this is quite achievable for anyone who wants it. Every further step in learning Hebrew will open new and new doors of knowledge of the Bible.

Even if you do not fully know the ancient Hebrew of Scripture, the BibleQuote program still offers some ways to get into the mysteries hidden in the depths of the original text.

Strong's Concordance, which contains all the words of the Bible and the number assigned to each of them, can also serve as an excellent tool for this.

Finding out the meaning of a word is extremely simple. The Strong's numbers in the program are clickable: click on a number, and the left column of the program shows the meanings of the word in the original text of Scripture, plus synonyms (with their numbers). (For more, see the videos "Strong's Numbers 1" and "Shofar and Trumpet".)

How to find all the verses in the Bible with a given word of the Hebrew original?

In the Russian translation of the Bible a Hebrew word is often translated in different ways. Thus the word KINOR is translated into Russian as "gusli" (psaltery), "cithara" and "harp". But the same word "cithara" is also used to translate another musical instrument, the "katirus" (the Greek kithara).

On the other hand, one and the same word in translation often stands for different words of the Hebrew original. For example, in the Synodal translation the word "artist" conceals several different Hebrew words:

1. חרש /harash/: craftsman, skilled master (engraver, artist, smith, carpenter).
2. חרש /heresh/: 1. craftsman, skilled master; 2. secretly.
3. יצר /yotser/: 1. to form, to sculpt; 2. to create, to fashion.
4. חכם /hakham/: 1. skilful, expert, experienced; 2. wise.
5. אמן /oman/: artist, skilled master.

In careful study of Scripture it is important to look into the nuances and to understand what is meant when this particular word is used rather than its synonym. All the meanings of the word "artist" in the Russian translation listed above are close to one another, but they express different sides of creativity. HARASH comes from working the material, drawing form out of the formless. HERESH comes from revealing what is hidden through creative work. YOTSER, from creating something new out of material. HAKHAM, from the wisdom of knowing God's creation. OMAN, the most mysterious name for the artist, is connected with faith and with the relationship with the Creator.

So how do you find all the verses in the Bible with the original Hebrew word, the very one you came across in a particular verse of Scripture? The BibleQuote program solves this easily: you search the program by Strong's number. To do this, type the number of the word you are looking for in the search box. (See the video below for details.)

How to find other words of the same root?

To get into the depth and the many meanings of the original text, it is not enough to know the translation of a particular word. A deeper level of meaning can be reached by studying other words of the same root as the word we are interested in. When we study other words of the same root and the contexts in which they are used, we discover something like a branching tree whose branches and leaves are connected and influence one another. So the text we are interested in may appear before us in a new, shimmering light, with its many meanings and different possible translations.

The method I want to share with you is quite simple and requires at least a basic knowledge of Hebrew (the deeper your knowledge, the deeper the searches you will be able to make).

Video in Russian

If, for example, you search by Strong's numbers for all the words with the same root as the word "amen", you get the following result:

Some of the words of the root «אמן»:

אמן (0539): 1. foster-father, nurse; 2. to be faithful, reliable, to be firm, unshakeable;

אמן (0540): to believe; participle: faithful, reliable.

אמן (0541): to turn to the right, to go to the right.

אָמָּן (0542): skilled master (artist).

אָמֵן (0543): 1. truth; 2. truly, surely, amen

אָמְנָה (0545): care, upbringing.

אָמְנָה (0546): surely, truly, indeed.

אֹמְנָה (0547): doorpost or pillar.

אֲמָנָה (0548): 1. agreement, covenant; 2. something fixed or established.

אׇמְנָם (0551): truly, surely, indeed, really.

לאֻמְנָם (0551): truly, surely, indeed, really, in very deed.

So what shall we say? If we want to go deeper, we will have to learn the language of Scripture at least a little (the more, the better). The better our command of Hebrew, the more Scripture will open up before us. It can perhaps be compared to looking through a microscope or a telescope: Scripture will unfold before us like the universe, and little by little we will learn to make out more and more stars in the sky of the text of the Tanakh.
