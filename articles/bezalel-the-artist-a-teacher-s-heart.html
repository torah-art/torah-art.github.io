Bezalel the Artist: A Teacher's Heart

2014 · Art Midrash, The Bible and the Artist

Bezalel could not help teaching; it had become his natural need, his calling.

Exod. 35:34 …and He hath put in his heart to teach…

וּלְהוֹרֹת נָתַן בְּלִבּוֹ

U-LEHOROT NATAN BE-LIBO

The ability to teach

In the previous posts, "The Calling to Teach", "The Parent" and "A Direct Hit on the Target", we began to look at the artist Bezalel's calling to TEACH, exploring the meaning of this charge from the Most High through the different meanings of the Hebrew word LEHOROT.

הורה [ORE]: parent
יורה [YORE]: archer
מורה [MORE]: teacher
תורה [TORAH]: teaching (08451)

On how to find words of the same root in the Hebrew text of Scripture, see here.

Teacher [MORE]: one who is filled with teaching and lives by what he teaches others. Teaching is what he breathes and lives by, his meaning. In Hebrew, TEACHING is TORAH.

17 Her ways are ways of pleasantness, and all her paths are peace.
18 She is a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her: and happy is every one that retaineth her.

Prov. 3:17–18

דְּרָכֶיהָ דַרְכֵי־נֹעַם וְכָל־נְתִיבוֹתֶיהָ שָׁלוֹם׃
עֵץ־חַיִּים הִיא לַמַּחֲזִיקִים בָּהּ וְתֹמְכֶיהָ מְאֻשָּׁר׃

DRAKHEHA DARKHEI-NOAM VE-KHOL-NETIVOTEHA SHALOM. ETS HAYIM HI LA-MAHAZIKIM BA VE-TOMKHEHA ME'USHAR

The tree of life. In the background of the original image: עֵץ חַיִּים הִיא.

To teach means to impart life, not to pass on formal knowledge.

A true teacher passes on not so much information, a set of rules, texts and laws, as life itself.

In this he becomes like God, whose nature is to give and to bless. So is the teacher. He shares the precious things that make up his very being, giving himself away.

מורה MORE, TEACHER: one who teaches by giving the pupil a target. This teaching gives him great strength to reach the target, like an arrow shot from a bow. MORE in some measure combines the parent and the archer, who gives the arrow its direction (the arrow is the pupil).

Sound-plays in the language (or Hasidic artistry)

Some interpreters pay attention to SOUND-PLAYS in Hebrew, that is, words of DIFFERENT roots that sound alike. It must be said at once that far from all specialists accept such interpretations. Hasidim and artists are the ones who do this most :). So the conclusions from the pairs of similar-sounding words below need not be taken too seriously. If you like, treat it as pure "artistry".

So close in sound to להורות [LEHOROT], to teach, is another word, לִראוֹת [LIROT], to see.

In this sound-play one can see a hint that the artist Bezalel was called to teach to see. For this is the most important quality of an artist: the ability to see (to hear, for a musician).

In the same word להורות [LE-OROT] (to teach) one can hear another word, as if inserted into it: אורות [OROT], lights (plural). Here one can see another hint: true teaching carries light within it.

So the teaching that God put into his heart carried a light whose source is the Creator. See also the genealogy of the artist Bezalel son of Uri (Uri, Heb.: the light of the LORD).

"He put in his heart…"

(Exod. 35:34): "And He hath put in his heart THE ABILITY TO TEACH." So the Synodal translation reads. The original Hebrew text has no word "ABILITY"; it says: "lehorot natan be-libo", "and TO TEACH He put in his heart…".

Clearly, that is not how one says it in Russian. But the smoother Synodal translation hides an important possible interpretation from us.

Having an ABILITY, we may use it or not. Our ability to sing, for example, does not necessarily mean we take up singing. We may sing, or not. Many people for one reason or another "bury" their talents, not thinking it important or necessary to use them.

Of Bezalel we read something different: teaching was part of his nature. God put it into his heart, moving it from the category of abilities into that of needs. Bezalel could not help teaching; it had become his natural need, his calling.

Interestingly, the well-known passage from the prophet Jeremiah, if read in Hebrew, almost repeats the passage about Bezalel.

In the Synodal translation: "I will put My law"; in Hebrew: "I will put My Torah (teaching)".

The nuance here is that in Bezalel's case God puts into his heart to teach (a verb), while the promise of the New Covenant speaks of teaching (a noun). But clearly, to be able to teach, the artist must first be taught.

"And He hath put in his heart to teach…" Exod. 35:34
וּלְהוֹרֹת נָתַן בְּלִבּוֹ		 "I will put My teaching (Torah) in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be My people." Jer. 31:33
נָתַתִּי אֶת־תּוֹרָתִי בְּקִרְבָּם וְעַל־לִבָּם אֶכְתְּבֶנָּה

So the passage about the gift of teaching given to Bezalel anticipates the prophet's words containing the promise of the New Covenant to all people. The artist Bezalel thus carried within himself a great prophecy for generations to come. It is probably no accident that it is an artist whom God gifts in this way, stressing the important role of his calling. Just as it is no accident that the greatest of the commandments, "ANOKHI ADONAI ELOHEKHA" (I am the LORD thy God), is followed by the commandment about creativity…

The essence of the word להורות [LEHOROT] (to teach) is beautifully and poetically expressed in David's words:

"Teach me (הוֹרֵנִי) Thy way, O LORD, and lead me in a plain path…" Ps. 27:11

הוֹרֵנִי יְהוָה דַּרְכֶּךָ וּנְחֵנִי בְּאֹרַח מִישׁוֹר

HORENI ADONAI DARKEKHA U-NEHENI BE-ORAH MISHOR

Video in Russian

It is interesting that in Bezalel's work God unites the artist and the teacher. God's command to the artist to teach points to the role and responsibility the Most High lays on the person of art and his creative work!

It says: "and He hath put in his heart that he may teach". This is about an ability that comes from his heart, which points to a connection with the Creator, realised through a special inspiration communicated through the heart. He taught to see and to hear something special: he taught to hear God, to see the Father creating. He taught artists a special relationship with the Creator.

We can speak of God's commandment for Israeli art: to serve by carrying light, proclaiming purity and holiness (see on this the genealogy of the artist, "Bezalel, Son of Light"). The artist is given authority to teach others, conveying through his creative work the revelation that has been opened to him.
