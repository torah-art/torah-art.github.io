The Bible and the Artist

Doing Good. Clothing Designer Lesya Repetivskaya

2016 · The Bible and the Artist

Lesya Repetivskaya, a clothing designer, stylist and God-given artist, shares her thoughts on expressing faith in her practice and in her business as a fashion designer.

Video in Russian

The video shows designs by Lesya Repetivskaya. Photos: Edik Boyko; editing: Ephraim Kalish.

In the period I call my early church, when I had only just come to faith and joined a community of believers, I found that some believers treated one another in a special way. For example, when two of them agreed on some service, one for the other, they behaved in a particular manner. Often, when I go to an acquaintance for a service, I expect and ask for a discount. But it was not so in this community: the service was not simply paid for; on top of that, the buyer would willingly add something over the agreed sum when settling up. It was beyond measure, beyond the agreement. In this way people wanted to show one another their love, which is like the love of God, because He shows us love and mercy beyond measure, not according to our merits, but because He loves us.

These people reasoned like this: if you are my brother, I should not ask you for a discount; on the contrary, I want to bless you beyond the agreement.

In my work I try to make the choice easier for my clients, for the women who order from me. To come to me and get the thing a young lady wants, she needs to buy the right fabric. Often this is very hard to do. So I take part in this process, although it is not in the price list. But it is very important to me that the result is a pleasant, good thing that can be worn for years, so that she remembers me and thanks God that our meeting took place. And the time I spend looking for the right fittings, the right material, the time when we look through many videos, magazines and photographs so that we hit the mark more precisely, is so that the person leaves me with a good thing, in a good mood and, without fail, in joy.

The hardest thing in my life is to draw the line between what I do from the heart and what I do by faith. I would like them to be one. And I often catch myself thinking that there is a dilemma: to give in for the sake of making my own work easier, or to stand up for my client's interests. For me this is the hardest thing. Because sometimes it is easier to agree with a person who insists on a project doomed to fail. It is much harder to argue with them. But out of a habit I have developed, to act by listening to my heart, I argue with the person, and sometimes I lose a client.

But for me it is important to remain a believer to the end, in the sense that you are not standing up for your own interest. The thing is that a person often cannot picture the final result, what they want. Say a woman has seen a beautiful dress on a pretty girl in a photo and is absolutely sure she will look exactly the same in the same dress. And to tell a mature lady that she has long ceased to be a girl, and that in this dress she will look more like a laughing-stock than an ornament to the town, sometimes takes courage. And here it is very important to convey unpleasant information in such a form that the person is not offended. For me this is the practical expression of my faith in life.
