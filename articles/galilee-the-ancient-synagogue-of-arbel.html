Galilee. The Ancient Synagogue of Arbel

2015 · Artifacts, Travels

The synagogue near the village of Arbel, a place also mentioned in the Tanakh, is special: it is the only one in all of Galilee whose entrance faces not south, towards Jerusalem, but east.

In Napoleon's time some believed that the Mount of Beatitudes was Mount Arbel, on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee, south of Capernaum. Mount Arbel hangs right over the Kinneret, north of the city of Tiberias. The mountain is 181 metres above sea level, or 400 metres above the Kinneret.

Every time I look over the ancient synagogues of Israel, I touch not simply history but the roots of my own family; I feel the unbroken chain of the generations of the Jewish people. It is always moving to walk where our fathers walked. I enter the house of prayer by the same entrance as they did, step up to the Aron ha-Kodesh, the place where the leather scrolls of the Torah were kept, turn to face an imagined congregation seated on the stepped benches around the walls, and together with them say our SHEMA.

The synagogue near the village of Arbel, mentioned in the Tanakh, is special: it is the only one in all of Galilee whose entrance faces not south, towards Jerusalem, but east. One explanation, a tradition, tells that two eminent rabbis of the time before the Babylonian exile, climbing the path up from Lake Kinneret, noticed a star unknown to them in the east, and took it as a sign that the Mashiach (Messiah) would come this way, from the east. The synagogue was later built here.

This entrance from the east awaits the coming of the King, just as the kos (the cup for the prophet on the Seder table) and the kise (a special chair at a brit milah) of Eliyahu await the prophet. My people are still waiting for our Messiah, and one day will meet Him with cries of "Baruch ha-ba!" and with mourning for the One who was pierced, every family apart. And Mashiach Yeshua will return to the mountains of Galilee, where His feet once walked. And who knows, perhaps He will yet visit this house of prayer and cry out our SHEMA…

The scholars who excavated here (the British in 1875 and the Germans in 1905) dated the building to the 2nd–4th centuries, but it undoubtedly stands on earlier foundations from the Second Temple period. The building was made of light-coloured stone and stood out well against the dark basalt buildings of the village. At the same time, local basalt was also used in some details of the building.

The synagogue at Arbel

Roland Kulessky. The Synagogue at Arbel (a line-by-line translation of the poem)

The face of millennia in the remains of the synagogue;
no verdict of death is read in them:
so a barrier stops the traveller, not the road,
so a relic does not lose its taste for life.
A skeleton in the embrace of horror; among the thorns
a gecko poses, ready to dart away in an instant,
and the stone of the body seems bent with pain,
and time is in no hurry… if only there were faith enough to wait
for deliverance from the east, entrusting oneself
to the prophet, flinging the entrance open:
the dispute with time is not bounded by a term
while the vault of heaven is not destroyed…
The Kinneret was shallow then, as now;
two men went up the mountain from it at dawn,
by the road to Arbel, hiding from the Philistines.
One asked: do you see, before me
a star from the east? What do you say, rabbi?
"The star of the Messiah; He is to be awaited from here.
The deeps of the Kinneret will open and swallow
His enemies!"
"When?"
"We can only guess…"
To come, to lay a hand on the stone,
to say to oneself: I endure and am ready to wait, as you are;
how one longs to become an example to one another…
if only for this, it is not worth burning bridges…

Departing from the rules, the synagogue was not built at the highest point of the village. The choice of site was evidently dictated by the terrain. Because of the slope of the mountain, the building stood as it were on two terraces. The central axis of the building ran from north to south. Its outer length was 20.35 m and its width 18.50 m. The façade was on the east, where the central entrance also was. Its doorway was 3.55 m high. It has survived well and delights visitors with its decoration of small circles. Above the lintel there was a window.

The prayer hall, 19 × 17.7 m, had the traditional three rows of columns arranged in the shape of the letter Π (a squared U), with heart-shaped corner columns. The columns had Corinthian capitals of various designs. On the east a courtyard adjoined the synagogue, surrounded by other buildings.
