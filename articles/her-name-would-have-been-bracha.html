Her Name Would Have Been Bracha

2015 · My Art Projects

אִיחוּל, תְּפִלָּה, הַבָּעַת מִשְׁאָלָה, הַבָּעַת רָצוֹן, הַבָּעַת כׇּל־טוּב, אֲמִירַת דְּבָרִים טוֹבִים

(a wish, a prayer, the expression of a desire, the expression of goodwill, the wishing of all good, the speaking of good words)

וְאֶעֶשְׂךָ לְגוֹי גָּדוֹל וַאֲבָרֶכְךָ וַאֲגַדְּלָה שְׁמֶךָ וֶהְיֵה בְּרָכָה׃

VE-E'ESCHA LE-GOY GADOL, VA-AVARECHECHA, VA-AGADLA SHMECHA, VE-HEYE BRACHA

BERESHIT (Gen. 12:2)

HER NAME WOULD HAVE BEEN BRACHA. Looking at a TILDA doll made by a craftswoman from Norway, I imagined what a Tilda would look like if she were made in Israel. Something like this… She would certainly have a majestic Jewish nose pointing the way. Her clothes would smell of the Bible. And her name would be BRACHA (blessing).

So the idea was born to make a series of such creatures under the Torah-Art brand, each of which would carry a particular message.

Time will tell whether this idea is destined to come to life.

2 And I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing:
3 And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed. (Gen. 12:2–3)

The word BRACHA (and the verb from it) occurs five times in these verses.

"Blessing" is a great name: the first chapter of the Torah describes the creation of the world, and the last chapter of the Torah is devoted to blessing. The greatest blessing is life, and the blessing of God in man: Moshe, the man of God, blessed the people of Israel at the end of his life.

The word BRACHA means: a wish, a prayer, the expression of a desire, the wishing of all good, the speaking of good words.
