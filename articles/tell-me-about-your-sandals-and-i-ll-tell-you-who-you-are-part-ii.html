Tell Me About Your Sandals and I'll Tell You Who You Are. Part II

2015 · Artifacts

In the era when Israeli statehood was taking shape, the most important accessory that defined the look of the "typical" citizen of the new country, along with shorts and the cloth hat, was the so-called "biblical sandals" (sandalim tanachiyim).
Classic Israeli sandals began to be produced in the 1960s by the shoemakers' cooperative Nimrod, which made this footwear extremely popular and a true cult object throughout the country. In Nimrod's product catalogues the sandals were described as "authentic Israeli footwear, almost unchanged since the time of the biblical patriarchs".

Translated from Hebrew and abridged by Ephraim Kalish (nrg.co.il).

Why did sandals become a symbol of the Zionist movement?

The identification of sandals with the national religious public happened almost instantly. This is no accident. As El-Or asserts, in national religious Zionism sandals actually strengthen the local, Israeli, secular side. In the many-sided self-identification of this sector of society, paratroopers' boots and worn sandals point to the specific, characteristic features of the Israeli as a citizen defending his country. A dati-tziyoni (religious Zionist) in sandals is marked by them as a citizen, a native of Israel who fights for his land, explores it on hikes, a family man: such is the image of the Israeli for the world.

It is also a political act. Sandals are part of the uniform, part of a wider work of taking hold of and settling the space of Eretz Israel.

El-Or asserts that sandals were adopted by religious Zionism, which seeks to keep away from all symbols of prestige, mainly because of their simplicity, functionality and modesty. The main argument for the success of sandals in the Israeli style is simplicity. In terms of style, everything heavy and cumbersome has disappeared over time, and only graceful and simple lines have remained.

"This line, adopted by the organisations of the socialists and the halutzim (as the pioneers are called in Israel), turned into a cultural hegemony, political and social. The course of the development of religious Zionism since 1967 (the stunning victory in the Six-Day War; in religious circles it was seen as nothing less than a miracle wrought by the Most High in six days: note by the Toraart editor) is an attempt to occupy a more significant position in all spheres of Israeli society and to inherit the hegemony and its symbols, all the values of the pioneers' settlement work, community and modesty. At first this process happened unconsciously. But later it became a conscious calling for religious Zionists to become part of the army and the mass media.

***

Today you will not find sandals, even in summer, among the secular young people of the elite circles living in the centre. Is this an attempt to separate themselves from religious society?

"Not only that. This process became noticeable at the beginning of the eighties, when the religious sector was not yet dominant. The Americanisation of the market began; shoes began to be imported, Reebok, which everyone wanted to have, wearing them even in summer. And now it seemed strange that for many years our unchanging footwear had been Nimrod sandals.

"Then came the period of rubber flip-flops. They have no buckles. Perhaps sandals are more suitable for the army, for long trips, and that is all. The original sandals came to be identified with the settlers. But students in Jerusalem, including those with left-wing views, still wear them, because in their eyes they are a symbol of what is Israeli, of Jerusalem; but Tel Aviv students no longer do. A non-religious young man will wear sandals only if he is a real chnun (in Russian such people are called 'botaniks', nerds).

"At the beginning of the eighties flamboyance came into fashion. At every step I met dolled-up women with red hair, in shiny leggings and wide flowery blouses. Only towards the end of the nineties, when the minimalist style became relevant, did people again begin to dress more simply." "Flamboyant outfits never became a mass trend in Israel," El-Or is convinced. "Simplicity and functionality remain relevant."

So who are the tourists who buy Israeli models of footwear today?
"It is important to remember that Jewish tourists from different countries today are to a great extent inclined to take an interest in Judaism, not necessarily Orthodox. These are people who send their children to Jewish educational institutions, or at least send them to Jewish summer camps. And then their daughters come here, to Israel, and know what is customarily worn among Israelis and want the same. Tourists also like to buy something that will make them like Israelis, natives of the country. Such a tourist wants to bring home from the trip something authentic, genuine."

What future awaits biblical sandals?

"Sandals really are a unique accessory," Tamar El-Or is sure. "Their history goes back thousands of years. And after the rebirth of Israeli statehood sandals found a new life here. The two crosswise straps became the most important attribute of the new Israeli, and they, I am sure, will not disappear."

***

From the Toraart editor: it moved me to read and translate this article. Because here is living history being made before our eyes. History conveyed in small details, through simple soles with two straps.

It is no accident that these sandals were part of a real boom, an explosion of interest among believers in many countries in what was happening at the moment of the proclamation, in 1948, of the independent Jewish State of Israel. And this process grew especially active with the beginning of Israel's miraculous victories in unequal wars with its many Arab neighbours. Many believers saw in this the hand of God and a confirmation of the Creator's words that He will not forsake His people and will one day gather them in the land of their fathers.

The article mentions sandals more than once as a symbol that holds a great deal, a symbol linking the unbroken chain of generations and serving, like tzitzit, as a reminder of the commandments of the Most High and of His promises to gather His people one day.

One more thing caught me strongly: God does His will as He pleases. He takes and calls into His plans whom He wishes, even a shoemaker. God speaks to His people through whatever He sees fit, even through the design of the simplest footwear, which has existed since ancient times. Through simple sandals He is free to stir the whole society of Israel, to encourage it and give it a leather symbol as a reminder of His glory, once revealed to our fathers, and a guarantee that today too Avinu shebashamayim, our Father in heaven, will not forsake us. Today He calls whoever He Himself wishes to create together with Him with the skill, the craft, the gift that we receive from above.
