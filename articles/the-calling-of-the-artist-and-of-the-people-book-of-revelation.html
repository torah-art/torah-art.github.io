The Calling of the Artist and of the People (Book of Revelation)

2018 · Art Midrash

Ephraim Kalish. A meeting with artists in Yerevan, Armenia, 2018.

To revive a people, you have to awaken its national consciousness. The proponents of spiritual (cultural) Zionism used to say that the Jew inside the Jew must wake up. In other words, he must understand that he is not living his life for nothing, but that God has called him to be a blessing to other nations.

For thousands of years Jews repeated the prayer LESHANA HABAA BIRUSHALAYIM (לְשָׁנָה הַבָּאָה בִּירוּשָׁלָיִם, next year in Jerusalem). But humanly speaking, it was impossible: how could a people, after thousands of years, return to the place where its Temple once stood? First under the Turks, and then in Mandatory Palestine, other people were already living on this land. Who would let the Jews in? Who would give them a state in their Promised Land? And yet they prayed this way for thousands of years. On the other hand, how do you persuade Jews scattered over the whole face of the earth to leave the places where they had settled and gather in a land that was mostly desert, with malarial swamps for the rest, and poverty and despair everywhere? For a Jew to truly believe his own prayer, he had to remember that he was a Jew.

That, by the way, is our paradox. We came from the Soviet Union, where we lived through seventy years of atheism. Until I was thirty, I did not consider myself a Jew. I knew it was written in my passport, and people around me sometimes reminded me of it in unpleasant ways. Only when I became a believer did my national consciousness awaken.

Now I want to speak to the Armenians. I know that God somehow manages to sustain your national consciousness: where it begins to fade, He fans it into flame again. I don't know how He does it, but it is very important. In Christian circles the words "there is neither Greek nor Jew" (Col 3:11) are often understood to mean that it doesn't matter who you are, as long as you love God. Of course, without love for God there is nothing to talk about. But how God looks at us is a separate question. I believe God sees us as individuals, as a small family, father, mother, and children, and as a large family, that is, a people. He places responsibility on peoples and gives them particular gifts.

The Book of Revelation says more than once that people from many tribes and nations will stand before the throne of the Most High (Rev 7:9). But one passage in the twenty-first chapter, one of the last in the book, is especially important. I will read it with a few omissions: "the glory of God did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof. And the nations of those being saved shall walk in the light of it: and the kings of the earth do bring their glory and honor into it... And they shall bring the glory and honor of the nations into it" (Rev 21:23–26).

The key words here are "and they shall bring the glory and honor of the nations into it." From them we understand, first, that distinct peoples will remain and that their uniqueness will not dissolve in God. Second, not every people will be able to enter the heavenly Jerusalem, only the one that brings its glory before the throne of the Most High. Remember: "none shall appear before me empty" (Exod 23:15). Every people must bring its glory and its honor.

The Greek word translated as glory means radiance. This is the radiance with which God illumines every people, just as Moshe's face shone when he spoke with God (Exod 34:29). The second word, honor, means price, value. These are the gifts that God gives to every people. The gifts differ, because we are different members of one body, and God gives each one its own calling.

The peoples entering the heavenly Jerusalem must bring radiance, the radiance that comes down on us when we commune with God face to face. We stand before Him, in this case as a people made up of saved persons, since there can be no saved people without saved persons. And the peoples bring their honor, their value, what God has given them, His gifts. They are to bring these gifts to life and be joined into one tree, the olive tree.

Jewish idealists believed that through culture, art, and the revival of the ancient Hebrew language and of national consciousness they could reach people's hearts. And they truly succeeded. The Bezalel School of Jewish Art in Jerusalem played a major role in this. Today there is not a single synagogue in the world where the signs it created, the emblems of the tribes of Israel and other symbols, are not present in one form or another. The school's artists showed their work at exhibitions in Europe and America. Where did they get these signs? From the Torah, of course: from the blessing of Yaakov, who blessed his twelve sons before his death (Gen 49), and from the blessing of Moshe, also given before his death (Deut 33). From these texts they worked out what the emblems of the tribes could look like. Artists and designers labored over this.

One example is the Sephardic synagogue in Jerusalem, built in 1912 and still standing today. We have been there: along the top of the wall runs a painted frieze with the emblems of the tribes, made by one of the students of the Bezalel School.

Don't think, "I'm just a fashion designer, what can I do?" A fashion designer is someone who carries a particular message. We have long since resigned ourselves to the world dictating its messages to us, because every piece of clothing expresses a worldview. But we, believing artists, often offer no alternative at all and don't even try, even though that is exactly our responsibility.

Armenia is once again at a crossroads. Artists, I am calling on you: even if you forget everything else I have said, remember this. Don't take my word for it; listen to God first of all. I am calling on you to offer an alternative, and I believe God expects this of you. Now, at this crossroads, foundations are being laid that people will live by afterward, perhaps for a long time. And the more truth there is in these foundations, the longer they will stand. I am speaking to you: fashion designers, performers, artists, musicians, chefs, poets.

How to preach through cooking, I don't know; ask God. A wedding photographer can capture the image of a couple getting married. Even if these people are complete unbelievers. But imagine that the photographer believes, prays, and asks God for an anointing, and then it is as if he is no longer the one taking the pictures, but God. And God never scans what is there with an accusing eye; He creates what He has conceived. And years later people look at these photographs, the Holy Spirit touches them, and they see God's gaze upon them through what the photographer did, an artist filled with the spirit.

On you, the artists, rests the responsibility for the awakening of the Armenian people, for its fulfilling its calling. Creativity and art are a mighty force. But Christianity often cuts itself down. Remember the expression "denying the power thereof" (2 Tim 3:5)? We use only a small part of the gifts and tools God has given us. He has given us much more, but we have cut it off, because we are afraid.

From the very beginning God had a design for every creative person. More than that, God sees every person without exception as an artist. I don't mean that everyone will paint landscapes and still lifes. I am talking about a creative personality that first of all changes itself. Such a person does not remake others; he changes himself, and gradually the atmosphere around him changes, so that others begin to want what he has, his faith. In this sense all Armenians are artists in their own way.

Let me remind those who don't yet know who Bezalel is: he is the very man God called to create the sanctuary of the Most High. Think about it: is the One who created heaven, earth, and man himself really unable to make Himself a worthy sanctuary? And yet He calls a man to build Him a sacred "little room," in which He essentially does not even intend to dwell, since the whole earth cannot contain His feet (Isa 66:1). But He sets a condition: VEASU LI MIKDASH VESHAKHANTI BETOKHAM (וְעָשׂוּ לִי מִקְדָּשׁ וְשָׁכַנְתִּי בְּתוֹכָם, and let them make Me a sanctuary, and I will dwell among them, Exod 25:8).

God places the responsibility on the artist, although he did not work alone. The meaning is this: make Me a sanctuary, and only then will I dwell among you. Think about what this says to us today. I am absolutely certain that these words are addressed to us as well. What is our role? Of course, they can be interpreted figuratively, since we are all creative people, and this is not only about art, but about creativity in the broad sense. And yet you, people of the arts, have a special calling. It is like prophecy: each of us can prophesy, but there is the special ministry of the prophet. So it is here: we are all creative people, but there is a special ministry, the mad ministry of the artist.
