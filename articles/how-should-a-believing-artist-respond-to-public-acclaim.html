How Should a Believing Artist Respond to Public Acclaim?

2015 · The Bible and the Artist

May the author of the article forgive me: I have changed his title. In the original it reads: "How Should a Pastor Respond to an Encouraging Word Said About His Sermon?" The subject seemed to me relevant and important for a believing artist endowed with talents. Strictly speaking, an artist by definition cannot be an unbeliever. But I mean those of us who believe that the gifts of heaven are not our own property and that we owe our creative work to the Almighty. For such an artist it is important to give all honour and glory to the Creator and not to yield by chance to the temptation of putting something into his own pocket. That is why I ventured on this substitution. Congregation leaders are not my field. But artists, yes indeed, starting with myself. I think our fellow artists will easily translate the pastor's situation in their minds into their own, when the ovations and the delight of the public ring out. So I have not touched the text of the article by a single comma.

How Should a Pastor Respond to an Encouraging Word Said About His Sermon?

Author: Brian Croft, practicalshepherding.com. Russian translation: Alex Fishman for ieshua.org. Translated here from the Russian.

"Great job... good sermon... that really spoke to me." The list of phrases a pastor may hear from people leaving after a church gathering could go on. Whether the sermon was good or not, these brief comments will be addressed to us with varying degrees of sincerity, and it is important that we know how to respond to them in a God-honouring way. Here are four recommendations:

1. Say "Thank you"

It is sad when a pastor tries to put on a false humility to hide his own insecurity or his inability to understand how to behave on hearing kind words about himself. As a rule this shows in the pastor's answer to a kind, encouraging comment, something like: "Oh... no, that sermon wasn't all that good. I forgot something there, and I lost my way here..." Just stop and say: "Thank you for your kind words."

2. Be grateful for the encouragement

Whatever the content of the comment, if it was meant to encourage you, give thanks for the words of encouragement and support. Be grateful, because a brief, simple or even superficial comment took someone's time to share these thoughts with you. Be grateful and simply receive it.

3. Be humble, so that the Lord may go on using you

What should humble us even more than listeners taking time to come up and encourage us after a sermon is the fact that God would choose such an imperfect vessel as us, week after week, Sunday after Sunday, to feed God's people with God's Word. This should make us marvel at every kind word said to us. When it stops touching us in this way, it is time to worry.

4. Give all the glory to God

The great temptation, when receiving compliments after a sermon, is to suppose that the fruit of the labour is all about us and because of us. When someone shares a kind word with you after a sermon, make sure that God is not forgotten but glorified. Not superficially or formally, but sincerely. We can give God glory with our lips but be filled with pride inside.

Suggested answers

In the light of the recommendations above, here are a few options that seem to me fitting for answering kind words after a sermon:

"Thank you for your kind words! God is good in the way He speaks to each of us through His Word."

"Thank you for your encouragement; I am grateful to God that He used His Word in this way today."

"Thank you for taking the time to share how God's Word touched you today. This passage moved me in a similar way too. God is so gracious..."

Pastors, receive the kind words addressed to you. Be encouraged by them. It will help you get through the discouragement that often comes to us on Mondays. And be humble, because in six days you will have to do it all again.

From the editor: For me this article was useful. There is something here to think about and to learn.
