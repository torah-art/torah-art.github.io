Biblical Feasts

When the Shofar Sounds, Listen!

2015 · Biblical Feasts

The prophet addresses all the ends of the universe, every nation and every person. The context of this prophecy speaks of God's judgements and of the restoration of Israel in the last times. The shofar appears here not as a Jewish folk tradition close to extinction, but as a great symbol and action for all who live on the earth.

"All ye inhabitants of the world, and dwellers on the earth, see ye, when he lifteth up an ensign on the mountains; and when he bloweth a shofar, hear ye."

Isa. 18:3

כָּל־יֹשְׁבֵי תֵבֵל וְשֹׁכְנֵי אָרֶץ
כִּנְשֹׂא־נֵס הָרִים תִּרְאוּ וְכִתְקֹעַ שׁוֹפָר תִּשְׁמָעוּ׃

KOL YOSHVEI TEVEL VE-SHOCHNEI ARETS, KI-NSO NES HARIM TIR'U VE-CHITKO'A SHOFAR TISHMA'U

Composer: Daniel Kruglov. From the cycle "The Weekly Torah Portions"

The correct translation: "…when the shofar sounds, listen!", VE-CHITKO'A SHOFAR TISHMA'U (you can hear the Hebrew pronunciation here).

What does LISTEN mean? What does NA'ASEH VE-NISHMA mean (Hebrew, literally: we will do and we will hear, Exod. 24:7)?

What action does the Most High expect from us when He says: "SHEMA YISRAEL!"? Something like: listen to the following information? Our correspondents report that the Lord is God?

No, He says: hear! Be obedient to what you have heard, obedient to My voice.

מִשְׁמַעַת (MISHMA'AT): obedience, discipline
מִשְׁמָע (MISHMA): hearing, listening, obedience, meaning, sense

שֵׁם (SHEM), a word of the same root, is not just a name a person hears and answers to. SHEM does something to a person if he is MUSHMA, obedient to it. A NAME is what (ideally) defines and expresses a person's calling, makes him what the Creator designed him to be.

"…when the shofar sounds, obey!"

The command to hear the voice of the shofar is the most important commandment of the feast of YOM TERUAH (the Feast of Blowing the Shofar, which tradition calls ROSH HA-SHANA). To hear the shofar is not simply not to stop one's ears, or to be near the one blowing it. TO HEAR means to prepare one's heart, to humble oneself before the Creator in obedience.

Festive shofar blowing in the messianic congregation "Ohalei Rachamim", Haifa, Israel. Ephraim Kalish (shofar), Shirley Mazin (violin), Rami Mazin (piano).
