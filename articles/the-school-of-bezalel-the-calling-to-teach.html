The School of Bezalel: The Calling to Teach

2014 · Art Midrash, The Bible and the Artist

Bezalel, the first Israelite artist, was called not only to build a sanctuary to God Most High in the wilderness of Sinai. There in the wilderness God also commands him to teach the people.

Bezalel: the calling to teach

Telling how the artist was filled with the Spirit of God, in wisdom, understanding, knowledge and every craft, the Torah also speaks of his calling to teach others, as it is written: "And He hath put in his heart that he may teach."

The question arises: what could the artist teach?

Evidently, what only an artist can teach! And what can he teach? Is it about the craft he had mastered? Was Bezalel to make artists of his brothers, who only yesterday had been treading mud and mixing it with straw to make bricks?

If you think about it, the question is not an idle one.

The Torah says that the whole building process, from collecting the offerings to the consecration of the finished, assembled and erected Tabernacle with all its furnishings, took six months.

This means the whole volume of work done by the craftsmen, with all the parts and furnishings of the Mishkan, including the holy garments of Aaron and his sons. That is, in these six months they had not only to train yesterday's mud-treaders in fine work, but at the same time to do all the work. On top of that, God insists (He comes back to this demand several times) that the work be done skilfully; that is, we are speaking of art of the highest level.

"And look that thou make them after their pattern, which was shewed thee in the mount."

וּרְאֵה וַעֲשֵׂה בְּתַבְנִיתָם אֲשֶׁר־אַתָּה מָרְאֶה בָּהָר׃

U-RE'E VA-ASE BE-TAVNITAM ASHER-ATA MAR'E BA-HAR

Exod. 25:40 and Exod. 27:8

We must also remember that the kinds of craft we are speaking of here include working precious stones, casting and working gold, silver and bronze, carving, making linen and splendid brocade with gold thread… Many of these specialities require serious training, experience and great skill.

Here is an example of the description of one of the works:

"And he [Bezalel] made the ephod of gold, blue, and purple, and scarlet [wool], and fine twined linen. And they did beat the gold into thin plates, and cut it into wires, to work it in the blue, and in the purple, and in the scarlet, and in the fine linen, with cunning work."

2 וַיַּעַשׂ אֶת־הָאֵפֹד זָהָב תְּכֵלֶת וְאַרְגָּמָן וְתוֹלַעַת שָׁנִי וְשֵׁשׁ מָשְׁזָר׃
3 וַיְרַקְּעוּ אֶת־פַּחֵי הַזָּהָב וְקִצֵּץ פְּתִילִם לַעֲשׂוֹת בְּתוֹךְ הַתְּכֵלֶת וּבְתוֹךְ הָאַרְגָּמָן וּבְתוֹךְ תּוֹלַעַת הַשָּׁנִי וּבְתוֹךְ הַשֵּׁשׁ מַעֲשֵׂה חֹשֵׁב׃

Exod. 39:2–3

Craftsmen capable of this studied for years and even decades, gaining experience and learning the secrets from father to son. Only in this way could they reach perfection in their work.

I heard the story of Abu Halab Bilal, a Syrian, the third generation to own a cloth shop in the Old City of Jerusalem. He says that today the production of the special artistic brocade with gold thread from the village of Tadmor (Syria) is very laborious and expensive work (tens of thousands of dollars per metre). Making one metre of the cloth takes forty days of work.

From all this it becomes clear that those whom Bezalel was to teach were already professionals and masters of a high level. Evidently, not only mud-treaders came out of Egypt…

Video in Russian

The children of Israel were Pharaoh's slaves, but they lived in their own towns and their own houses, kept households, raised cattle, practised crafts and so on; and besides that they had a duty of forced labour: making bricks for Pharaoh's vast building works.

And if all this is so, we must again ask: what, then, did Bezalel teach the craftsmen?

If our thesis is right that not only unskilled slaves came out of Egypt but also masters of their crafts, we must work out what, in that case, the Most High charged Bezalel to teach them.

Apparently he taught them what is not directly connected with technique and technology. He taught them something far more essential and deep.

I think he taught them to see and to hear, beyond even the usual artist's ability to see proportion, colour, image, in short, what an ordinary person does not see. These two abilities, to "see" and to "hear", to perceive not only the physical earthly reality but also the higher heavenly one. Without this they would simply have been unable to embody images of heavenly things in concrete, tangible earthly works. They had to become sensitive to the perfection of God's creation in order to meet the requirements of the Creator of heaven.

The second thing Bezalel had to teach was inspiration. The creativity to which God called Israel is co-authorship with the Spirit of God: human beings creating together with the Creator of the universe.

In all likelihood, this work required the gifts that Bezalel received and that the Creator proclaimed before all Israel when He chose him. So that Bezalel could create and teach such great things, God fills him with the Spirit of God.

"And I have filled him with the Spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship… and in the hearts of all that are wise hearted I have put wisdom, that they may make all that I have commanded thee."

וָאֲמַלֵּא אֹתוֹ רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים בְּחָכְמָה וּבִתְבוּנָה וּבְדַעַת וּבְכָל־מְלָאכָה׃ …
וּבְלֵב כָּל־חֲכַם־לֵב נָתַתִּי חָכְמָה וְעָשׂוּ אֵת כָּל־אֲשֶׁר צִוִּיתִךָ׃

Exod. 31:3–6

VA-AMALE OTO RUAH ELOHIM BE-HOKHMA U-VINA U-VE-DAAT U-VE-KOL MELAKHA… U-VE-KOL HAKHAM-LEV NATATI HOKHMA VE-ASU ET KOL-ASHER TSIVITIKHA

So what shall we say: what did Bezalel teach his fellow tribesmen and colleagues?

Let us suppose this: God wanted Bezalel to share with others the gifts of heaven with which the Most High had filled him.

And they had to learn a special relationship with the Spirit of God, so as to be able to bring heavenly things down to earth through creative work. We will try to explore this in the following posts.
