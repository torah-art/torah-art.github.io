Israel and the Church

Let Each Remain in His Calling

2014 · Israel and the Church

"Let every man abide in the same calling": Jews as Jews and Greeks as Greeks. How, then, are we to understand the words of the apostle Paul that follow: "circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing…" (1 Cor. 7:19)?

17 … as God hath distributed to every man, as the Lord hath called every one, so let him walk. And so ordain I in all churches. 18 Is any man called being circumcised? let him not become uncircumcised. Is any called in uncircumcision? let him not be circumcised. 19 Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of the commandments of God. 20 Let every man abide in the same calling wherein he was called. 24 Brethren, let every man, wherein he is called, therein abide with God. (1 Cor. 7:17–24)

If "every man abides in his calling", Jews as Jews (that is, circumcised) and Greeks as Greeks (that is, uncircumcised), how are we to understand the words that follow: "circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of the commandments of God" (1 Cor. 7:19)?

Should Jews be circumcised, or is it really no longer worth it, if our circumcision is now NOTHING? Is there a contradiction here? How can it be resolved?

Why does Paul in one place insist on circumcision for Jews and uncircumcision for Greeks, ORDAINING this in ALL CHURCHES?
Could it be like this: he told the Jews to remain in circumcision only for a time (well, because of their weakness), until the last times came? But he believed that he was already living in the very last times.

If we read it again carefully, we come up again and again against Paul's insistent demand that each one remain in the calling in which he was called, just as he is. Four times, with enviable persistence, he repeats the phrase: "as each one was called… let him remain". He insists on it, putting it as a command: "as God hath distributed to every man, as the Lord hath called every one, so let him walk. And so ORDAIN I in ALL CHURCHES" (1 Cor. 7:17).

A doubt arises: could Paul have said one thing to the Jews of his day and meant something quite different about those to come (as if he said it with his fingers crossed in his pocket, "write down two, carry one in your head")?

This apparent contradiction can be resolved without accusing Paul of inconsistency, tricks, cunning and the like. When he says "circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing", he is telling the Jews: "As far as obtaining salvation goes, it means nothing at all! Your circumcision does not bring you one iota closer to God!" And to the Greeks: "Your uncircumcision does not take you one iota further from God!" That is, as far as drawing near to God goes, it makes no difference at all whether you are circumcised or not. In this sense circumcision and uncircumcision are NOTHING! They have no effect on God's attitude to you, His love or His mercy!

Then he says: "…but the keeping of the commandments of God". That is, IN THEMSELVES neither circumcision nor uncircumcision means anything at all before God! What really is valuable and important to Him is our obedience to His commands (commandments).

And He said to some: be circumcised! This is an everlasting ordinance for you throughout your generations (Gen. 17:9–11): "And God said unto Abraham, Thou shalt keep my covenant therefore, thou, and thy seed after thee in their generations. This is my covenant, which ye shall keep, between me and you and thy seed after thee; Every man child among you shall be circumcised. And ye shall circumcise the flesh of your foreskin; and it shall be a token of the covenant betwixt me and you."

And to others He commanded: do not be circumcised (this command was spoken by the Spirit of God through the apostles at the council in Jerusalem in AD 52; see Acts 15). So from some He expects circumcision, in obedience to His commandment, and from others uncircumcision, in the same obedience. What is required of each one is obedience to the commandment, not arguments about whether circumcision is useful and, if it is, why it is not for everyone.

This brings to mind the passage in the Gospel of John where Yeshua tells Peter by what death he will glorify God, and "Peter, turning about, seeth the disciple whom Jesus loved following… Peter seeing him saith to Jesus, Lord, and what shall this man do? Jesus saith unto him, If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee? follow thou me" (John 21:19–22).

The principle here is important: God may command one to die for His glory and another to live for that same glory, loving both equally. So we should not be troubled about why it is one way for some and another way for others, rather than the same for all; why the Spirit of God decided through the apostles one thing about believers from the nations and another about the Jews. "Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of the commandments of God."

"But wait, wait," the attentive student of Scripture will say, "Paul himself discusses whether circumcision is useful or useless, and comes to a clear conclusion…"
"What advantage then hath the Jew? or what profit is there of circumcision?" (Rom. 3:1)

Two questions about the advantage of a calling, and two answers: why circumcision has an advantage "much every way", and on the other hand no advantage at all, since "both Jews and Gentiles… are all under sin".

Notice that for each statement Paul gives arguments that are astonishing and have not lost their force. That is, the counter-arguments do not cancel them in any way.

ARGUMENTS: God is faithful. Not even man's unfaithfulness and falsehood can cancel His faithfulness.

COUNTER-ARGUMENTS: "by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight". That is, if you think that you are a Jew (circumcised) and expect to be justified by that while you go on sinning, know that you have no advantage over a sinner just like you who is only uncircumcised. Your circumcision does not save you in any way… and keeping the commandments of the law does not bring you any closer to God.

Likewise, for a Jew to fulfil his calling from the Lord, there is a great advantage in his keeping every command of His, including circumcision. It is a great advantage for each of us (whether Jew or Greek does not matter) to keep God's commands (commandments) as exactly as possible. But there is no advantage in circumcision or uncircumcision if we try to earn salvation or win love through them. Salvation and God's love come by faith alone! We cannot be justified before Him by any works, only by faith!

"What advantage then hath the Jew? or what profit is there of circumcision? 2 Much every way: chiefly, because that unto them were committed the oracles of God. 3 For what if some did not believe? shall their unbelief make the faith of God without effect? 4 God forbid: yea, let God be true, but every man a liar; as it is written, That thou mightest be justified in thy sayings, and mightest overcome when thou art judged.
***
9 What then? are we better than they? No, in no wise: for we have before proved both Jews and Gentiles, that they are all under sin; 10 As it is written, There is none righteous, no, not one: 11 There is none that understandeth, there is none that seeketh after God. 20 Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin. 21 But now the righteousness of God without the law is manifested, being witnessed by the law and the prophets; 22 Even the righteousness of God which is by faith of Jesus Christ unto all and upon all them that believe: for there is no difference: 23 For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God; 24 Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus. 28 Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law. 29 Is he the God of the Jews only? is he not also of the Gentiles? Yes, of the Gentiles also: 30 Seeing it is one God, which shall justify the circumcision by faith, and uncircumcision through faith. 31 Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law." (Rom. 3:1–31)

***

What, then, is the reason for Paul's demand that the Jews go on being circumcised and the Greeks not touch circumcision?

When Paul speaks here of the circumcision of some and the uncircumcision of others, he means the CALLING of the Jews (the circumcised) and the CALLING of the Greeks (the uncircumcised).

"But wait," a watchful brother will say, "it is written in Col. 3:10–11: 'And have put on the new man, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him: 11 Where there is neither Greek nor Jew, circumcision nor uncircumcision, Barbarian, Scythian, bond nor free: but Christ is all, and in all.' And also in Gal. 3:28: 'There is neither Jew nor Greek…'" True! But then let us read to the end: "…there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus." Who, being sober and of traditional orientation, would seriously claim today that there really is no difference between a man and a woman? Did you smile? Good.

A Jew smiles too when people start explaining to him that he is now a Greek. Of course, as far as salvation goes, a man has no advantage over a woman. God does not love him more than her. In that sense there is neither one nor the other for Him. But as far as calling goes, there is a great difference between husband and wife. And glory to the Creator for that difference!

So the husband and the wife, the Jew and the Greek, each have their own calling. For both it is beautiful, precious and important before the Creator! The Father looks at His children, so different, yet none the less beloved for that. He wants us to be happy, so that EACH of us fulfils HIS OWN CALLING, the purpose, the reason He brought us (gave us birth) into this world. And He has endowed each one with gifts of his own, belonging to him alone…
