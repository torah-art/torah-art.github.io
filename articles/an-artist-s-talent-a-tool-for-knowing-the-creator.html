The Bible and the Artist

An Artist's Talent: A Tool for Knowing the Creator

2015 · The Bible and the Artist

The original, highest purpose of creativity is knowing the Creator…

The artist's talent

The original, highest purpose of creativity (and of art) is knowing the Creator, communion with Him through joint creation, a holy service before the Most High. This is where Adam began: the first thing said about him is that he began to create and change reality, as described in the Torah (Gen. 2:5 and Gen. 2:19–20).

Many significant artists, reflecting on their work, compare it to prayer and holy service before the Creator. Joseph Brodsky believed that poems not meant for God's ears make no sense. Stepan Erzia compared entering his studio to the holy service of a priest entering the Holy of Holies to minister in the Temple.

Our co-creation with God cannot enrich or help Him in any way. He is not "worshipped with men's hands, as though he needed any thing" (Acts 17:25). But creativity helps us draw near to Him through His inspiration. Through CO-creation with Him we are united with Him, purified and perfected in His presence…

God gives the artist a talent so that, using it as a tool, he may come to know the Creator, exploring and interpreting His word in spirit and in truth. Sometimes this tool, the talent, serves as a spade and a pickaxe; sometimes as tweezers and a scalpel; sometimes as a magnifying glass and a microscope, a spyglass and a telescope.

It is important to see what is written in the Bible from a bird's-eye view as well, taking in great expanses at a glance. And to go deep into it one needs an aqualung and a diver's suit. And, of course, where the TANAKH is concerned, one cannot do without the KINOR (the harp) to open the gates of heaven, as is written in 2 Kings 3:15.

Does a believer need to learn an art, or will anything do?

One often meets the mistaken idea that all you need to become such a harpist is to love God with all your heart… and that's all. Can't one simply commune with Him as one is?

Of course, God by His own will can speak to the mountains and open the mouth of a donkey. But the Levites in the Temple constantly practised and perfected their craft.

The sons of Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun, for example, studied in order to prophesy with harps, psalteries and cymbals. The sons of Asaph, under his direction, played according to the order of the king. The sons of Jeduthun, under his direction, played the harp to give thanks and praise to the LORD. The sons of Heman, the king's seer in the words of God, lifted up the horn with cymbals, psalteries and harps for the service of the house of God, according to the king's order, or that of Asaph, Jeduthun and Heman (1 Chron. 25)…

Why should we develop the talents and gifts God has given, learn and improve? Perhaps all this was necessary only in the past?

To hear great classical music, for example, one has to learn to listen to it.

The prophet Shmuel, to hear and recognise the voice of God, learned to listen. He was taught by Eli, who ministered before God and served at the tabernacle (1 Sam. 3).

David began his career as king and commander with a "music school". His work as a shepherd left him free time while the flock grazed. All that time he practised on the kinor. He had a great deal of practice, many hours of training and experience of seeking inspiration from the Most High. And when King Shaul needed a gifted musician who could soothe and heal his affliction by playing, David was already widely known for his musical skill (1 Sam. 16:18). And his gift was so developed and strong that the evil spirit departed when David took up the kinor.

***

Do I mean that those of us who did not go to art school have no chance of drawing near to the Creator? Not at all! I mean that ALL of us are called to be artists and poets. That is what is said of a person who does something with inspiration: he is an artist (a poet) in HIS OWN work.

Author: Ephraim Kalish
