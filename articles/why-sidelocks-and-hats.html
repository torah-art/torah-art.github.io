Israel and the Church

Why Sidelocks and Hats?

2015 · Israel and the Church

Editor's note: those whom Israelis call "knitted kippot" (the name itself shows that they do not wear hats), for example, dress more freely. This article is rather about the ultra-Orthodox. But in any case it is interesting and important to understand…

Source: toldot.ru. Author: Miriam Klimovskaya. Translated from Russian.

And what about my unique individuality?

Why do observant Jews dress alike? Why such "squareness"? After all, one can look respectable in a white suit and without a hat. Why is no room left for individuality?

By way of introduction, here are two stories from the not very distant past of our people.

The beginning of the 19th century. The reign of Nicholas I is in full swing: the same Nicholas who issued the decree on the cantonists. That terrible decree overshadowed an endless series of others produced by the tireless imagination of the despot and those around him. One of them was a ban on… wearing traditional Jewish clothing, beards and sidelocks. The penalty for disobedience: Siberia and the convict companies. Soldiers and policemen, glad of an easy job, catch Jews by their long coats, cut off their sidelocks, pull the wigs off women. And the Jews? They try with all their strength to save themselves. They pay huge bribes, hide in their houses, cover their beards with scarves and pray for the decree to be revoked.

The middle of the 20th century. The world has gone mad. The Nazi monster rules most of Europe. The Jews, beaten, robbed and humiliated, are locked in ghettos. And decree after decree rains down on them, each more inhuman than the last. Among them is a ban on wearing beards and sidelocks. The penalty for disobedience: death. Many Jews sigh, "danger to life", and shave off their beards in tears. But there are whole groups, in particular young Gur Hasidim, who declare: this is a "gezerat shmad", an attempt to put an end to Jewry; we must not agree; we will die but not give in! And they hide in bunkers, where they go on studying Torah, sharing their meagre food with one another, and keep their Jewish appearance.

What made Jews in every age fight desperately to keep a specifically Jewish appearance? And why did this appearance so irritate those who hated the Jews?

There is a halakhah: Jewish clothing must differ from the clothing of the surrounding peoples, so that a Jew can be seen from afar. But must it be the same for all? Hasidim of different movements and the old "Yerushalmi" families faithfully keep the authentic appearance of their grandfathers, formed over hundreds of years. Those who know can tell unerringly by the colour of the stockings, the material of the coat and the shape of the shtreimel: this is a Yerushalmi, this is a Hasid of such-and-such a rebbe, this is an unmarried young man, and this one is already married. The clothing of "Lithuanian" Jews differs greatly from Hasidic dress and is more like European clothing, but it still stands out sharply from the motley crowd around.

To some champions of "individuality" the similar clothing of the Haredim recalls an army uniform. We are not offended by this analogy; on the contrary, it seems very apt. A Jew faithful to the Torah knows that our life in this world is an endless war with our evil inclination, the yetser ha-ra. That is individual; and together the Jewish people fights (ideally) for the glorification of the name of the Almighty. Just as soldiers of different arms of the service wear their own special uniform, so believing Jews wear a special uniform that differs from one movement to another. In each of them the common enemy, the forces of impurity, is fought with its own kind of weapon: in one more stress is laid on Torah study, in another on prayer. The same uniform lets soldiers recognise one another from afar, disciplines them and helps them fight the enemy more effectively. That is why a Jew's appearance so irritates antisemites, who would like the Name of the Creator not to be present in this world.

In the time of the Beit ha-Mikdash the kohanim and Levites on active service in the Temple wore the same uniform: the kohanim theirs, the Levites theirs. Now that the Beit ha-Mikdash is destroyed, every Jew faithful to the Torah is on active service: bringing near the Geulah, the Redemption. Unfortunately, we are few, and the enemy keeps attacking. Every warrior counts. In time of war, if a soldier takes off his uniform and disappears into the crowd, he will be considered a deserter, however he may justify himself…

A few words about individuality. Everyone knows the saying: "People meet you by your clothes and see you off by your mind." If we understand "mind" in a wider sense, as each person's personal qualities, it is clear why they "see you off by your mind". To get to know a person well, one must spend some time with him, talk with him, test him in various situations. Then we shall see his individuality. But "they meet you by your clothes". A person's appearance expresses what can be learned about him at first sight: for example, what convictions he shares (how deep they are is already a question of his "individuality"). A Jew's appearance expresses his faithfulness to the ideals of the Torah and his readiness to follow them in any circumstances; at the very least, his wish to be seen in just that way. This is the exceptional individuality of the Jewish people as one whole.

And what about each person's own individuality? Our task is to bring the light of the Almighty into the world. For that one must cleanse one's soul as fully as possible of foreign admixtures. Moshe Rabbeinu was found worthy to bring the Torah to the people of Israel because he cleansed himself completely of every admixture and became a pure conductor of the will of the Almighty. For this he needed long years of intense individual spiritual work. Each of us stands before an individual field of work on himself. He has to root out his individual weeds and thorns and grow his individual harvest.

In conclusion, a few words to the champions of "individuality in clothing". One may ask: are they ready to go out into the street in such an "individual" outfit that all the passers-by will stop, gape, and then for a long time tap their fingers on their temples? If so, then of course there are no further questions. But as a rule most people dress (or undress) as is customary in the circle to which they count themselves. A Jew in a white suit (in our time) may look "respectable" like a Hollywood star or a financial magnate from Dallas, but not like a Jew who seeks to do the will of the Creator.

As a rule, people who think too much about their appearance try to copy someone more successful than themselves. But we, concerned with our inner growth, try to imitate our sages and righteous men: their prayer, their Torah study and their daily deeds.
