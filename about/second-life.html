TORAART: A Second Life — How AI Is Helping Rebuild Ten Years of Work

For more than ten years, I worked on TORAART, a website devoted to exploring the relationship between the Bible, creativity, and the arts. Over time, it grew into a collection of nearly 200 articles, studies, and reflections on biblical figures, music, artistic expression, and the calling of the artist.

One question has always been at the heart of this project: What does the Bible itself have to say about creativity? Not simply what we have been taught to believe about it, or what familiar interpretations tell us, but what we can actually discover by paying close attention to the biblical text. Sometimes a fresh reading of a familiar passage opens an entirely unexpected perspective.

Over the years, TORAART became something of a personal library — a place where decades of questions, discoveries, observations, and conversations gradually came together. It was more than a collection of articles. It reflected an ongoing exploration of the relationship between human creativity and the Creator.

When a Library Disappears

Then the website went offline. Technical problems made it inaccessible, and I found myself facing the daunting task of rebuilding what had taken more than a decade to create. Articles, illustrations, links, categories, and the connections between them all needed attention. Much of the original structure had to be reconstructed.

Of course, the ideas themselves had not disappeared. But having scattered texts and files is not the same as having a functioning library where readers can explore subjects, follow connections, and discover something they weren't originally looking for. I soon realized that simply recreating the old website would not be enough. This was also an opportunity to rethink and improve it.

An Unexpected Assistant

This is where artificial intelligence entered the story. Not long ago, I would hardly have imagined using AI to help rebuild an entire website. Yet it has made possible a restoration process that would otherwise have required far more time and technical effort.

And something interesting happened along the way. AI proved useful not only for technical work but also for organizing years of accumulated material. What began as a restoration project gradually became an opportunity to reconsider the whole collection. Which articles were still relevant? Which needed revision? Which were better left behind? How could the website become easier to navigate, especially for someone encountering these ideas for the first time? The project was no longer simply about recovering the past. It was becoming a way to prepare for the future.

Of course, AI cannot replace the author. It cannot decide what I intended to say ten years ago, which conclusions I still stand by, or what I believe deserves to be shared today. Those decisions — and the responsibility that comes with them — remain mine. But AI can help recover, organize, and give new life to work that took a human being years to create.

The Doors Are Open Again

Today, TORAART is back online. The website has been reorganized, navigation has improved, and articles are gradually finding their places in a renewed structure. The work is far from finished. Some older articles have been retired, others are being revised, and new studies and reflections will continue to appear.

For search engines, this is essentially a new website. It will take time for it to become visible again and find its readers. But the most important thing has already happened: the library is open again.

An Invitation to Explore

I have never intended TORAART to be a collection of final answers. I see it more as an invitation to return to Scripture, ask questions, examine familiar passages carefully, and sometimes discover things we may have overlooked.

Why was Bezalel, an artist, the first person of whom God explicitly said, "I have filled him with the Spirit of God"? Why does the Torah preserve the name of Jubal, the father of all who play the harp and flute? What place does artistic creativity occupy in the biblical understanding of humanity and its relationship with the Creator? These are just a few of the questions explored on TORAART.

My hope is that the website will become more than a restored digital archive. I hope its articles will continue to find readers, encourage thoughtful conversations, raise new questions, and perhaps inspire discoveries of their own.

If you find something meaningful here, please consider sharing it with friends, fellow artists, Bible study groups, or others who might be interested. A personal recommendation — even a few words about what caught your attention — can make a real difference.

After all, the point of rebuilding a library is not merely to preserve its books. It is to make sure they can be read again.
